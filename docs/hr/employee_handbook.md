# VeeWash / WashPro Employee Handbook

**VeeWash LLC**  
Effective date: _______________

This handbook applies to **W-2 employees** and **temporary W-2 employees** working at VeeWash / WashPro laundry operations.

Read this handbook. Ask your supervisor if anything is unclear. Policies here are meant to keep work safe, fair, and consistent for everyone.

---

## 1. Welcome

You are part of a team that handles customer laundry with speed, accuracy, and care. This handbook explains how we work, how performance is measured, and what happens when standards are not met.

**Temporary employees:** You are held to the same operational standards as other employees. Your assignment may end when business needs change, when the assignment period ends, or when standards are not met.

---

## 2. Employment relationship

- Employment with VeeWash LLC is **at-will** where applicable by law. Either party may end the relationship according to law and any written terms that apply to your role.
- You must follow this handbook, supervisor instructions, and posted safety rules while on duty.
- Job duties may include lifting and moving laundry bags, loading machines, sorting, folding, pushing carts, standing for long periods, and repetitive handling tasks. Tell your supervisor if you need a reasonable accommodation.
- Maintain accurate contact information in company systems so we can reach you for scheduling and official communications.

---

## 3. Official company communications

Employees agree that **company-provided email**, **personal email on file**, **company software notifications**, **text messages**, and **written notices** may be used for:

- Scheduling  
- Payroll communications  
- Policy updates  
- Coaching  
- Warnings  
- Performance discussions  
- Assignment changes  
- Separation notices  

You are responsible for **maintaining current contact information** and **reviewing communications** sent through these approved channels. Missing a message because you did not check approved channels does not excuse failure to respond when a response is required.

---

## 4. Schedule, attendance, and reliability

**Attendance & Reliability** is a core performance category.

- **Arrive on time** for scheduled shifts. Being ready to work at start time means clocked in and at your assigned station unless your supervisor assigns prep elsewhere.
- **Follow the call-out procedure:** Contact your supervisor as early as possible before a missed shift. If you cannot use the usual channel, use the next approved channel (text or call) and document that you tried. Unapproved absences are serious.
- **No-call / no-show** (missing work without notice) is a **serious violation** and may result in discipline up to immediate separation.
- **Tardiness:** First late → coaching email; second late → warning email; continued pattern → separation at management discretion.
- **Accept assigned work** within your role. Refusing assigned tasks without a valid safety or operational reason may be treated as a performance and conduct issue.
- **Clock in and out accurately** using the company time system. Do not clock for another person or ask another person to clock for you.

Temporary employees: ending your assignment for schedule or business reasons is not necessarily a disciplinary separation, but the same attendance standards apply while you are scheduled.

---

## 5. Pay and timekeeping

- You are paid according to your hire documentation, wage notice, and applicable law.
- **Clock in** when you begin work and **clock out** when you finish, including breaks taken according to supervisor and policy direction.
- Review your hours and pay records. Report errors to your supervisor or payroll promptly.
- Direct deposit and other pay methods are set up during onboarding. Keep banking information current.
- Overtime and break rules are explained by your supervisor and follow applicable law and company practice.

Payroll questions are operational communications and should use approved channels (email, text, or in-app as directed).

---

## 6. How performance is measured

Performance is evaluated in **five categories**. Your supervisor may discuss one or more categories in coaching, warnings, or other feedback.

| Category | What it means |
|---|---|
| **1. Attendance & Reliability** | On time, present, call-outs handled properly, accepts assigned work |
| **2. Productivity** | Meets department productivity expectations for your role |
| **3. Quality** | Sorting, folding, processing, and bag handling meet standards with low rework |
| **4. Customer Item Care** | No lost items, no mixed orders, garments and linens handled properly |
| **5. Conduct & Professionalism** | Follows instructions, respectful behavior, safe and professional conduct |

**Specific productivity and quality metrics** are established by management in **Performance Settings and operational systems** — not in this handbook. The signed **Performance Standards & Benchmarks Addendum** defines performance expectations; numeric targets may change without a new signature.

Consistent problems in any category may lead to coaching, a warning, and separation.

---

## 7. Job standards — laundry operations

These standards describe **expectations**. **Attendance policies** and qualitative performance expectations are in the **Performance Standards & Benchmarks Addendum**. **Numeric targets** are set in management systems and may change without a new signed document.

### Productivity

- Stay engaged in assigned work during clocked time.
- Complete sorting, washing support, drying support, weighing, folding, and bag handling at the **pace required for your department**.
- Rush and priority orders get appropriate priority without sacrificing quality or safety.
- Sustained underperformance after coaching may lead to further discipline.

### Quality

- Sort items correctly by customer, order, and service type.
- Fold and present items according to training and posted standards.
- Label and bag orders correctly. Double-check tickets, tags, and weights when required.
- Rework (fixing mistakes) should be rare. Repeated rework is a quality issue.

### Customer item care

- Treat every garment and linen as if it were your own.
- Keep customer orders **separated** at all times. Mixed orders are a serious issue.
- Report damaged, wet, stained, unusual, or questionable items immediately — do not hide problems.
- Do not remove customer property from the premises except as directed for your role.
- Do not make unauthorized promises to customers about refunds, timing, or credits.

### Safety on the floor

- Work safely around machines, chemicals, hot surfaces, carts, and wet floors.
- Wear appropriate attire and follow hygiene expectations for food-adjacent and customer-facing work.
- Report spills, leaks, smoke, burning smells, broken equipment, and injuries **immediately**.

---

## 8. Conduct and professionalism

**Conduct & Professionalism** applies on the floor, in break areas, and when representing the company.

- Follow supervisor and manager instructions for work flow, quality, and safety.
- Treat coworkers, supervisors, and customers with respect. No threats, harassment, intimidation, or abusive language.
- No fighting, sleeping on duty, loitering, or disruptive behavior.
- Personal phone use should not interfere with work or safety. Follow posted phone rules.
- Use company equipment, supplies, and systems only for work — not personal laundry or outside jobs without approval.
- No alcohol, illegal drugs, or impairment on premises. No weapons or dangerous items unless required by law.
- Keep work areas reasonably clean. Food and drinks stay away from customer garments and production surfaces.
- Follow access, security, and restricted-area rules.

---

## 9. Safety and incident reporting

- Report injuries, near-misses, unsafe conditions, chemical exposure, and customer-property incidents **immediately** to a supervisor.
- Cooperate with incident documentation and any required follow-up.
- Use fire extinguishers and emergency equipment only in real emergencies when you can do so safely. Call 911 when needed.
- Do not bypass machine safety features or operate equipment you are not trained to use.

Safety issues may be logged internally and may result in discipline if caused by negligence or rule violations.

---

## 10. Performance documentation

**You do not sign separate forms** for coaching, warnings, attendance discussions, performance discussions, or separation.

Management documents performance internally and may rely on:

- Emails and text messages sent through approved channels  
- System notes and HR timeline entries  
- Productivity and quality reports  
- Attendance and time records  
- Payroll records  
- Customer complaints  
- Manager observations  

Coaching and warnings may be delivered **verbally** and/or through **approved written channels** listed in Section 3. What matters is that standards are clear and issues are addressed — not a stack of signed HR forms.

---

## 11. Discipline process

When standards are not met, management generally follows these steps:

| Step | Action |
|---|---|
| **1** | **Coaching** — Clear explanation of the issue, expectation, and how to improve |
| **2** | **Warning** — Written or documented notice through approved channels that continued problems may lead to separation |
| **3** | **Separation** — End of employment or assignment |

Management **may skip steps** for serious misconduct, including but not limited to:

- Theft or suspected theft  
- Violence, threats, or harassment  
- Falsifying time, records, or documentation  
- Intoxication or impairment on duty  
- Serious safety violations  
- Intentional damage to company or customer property  

Separation and other notices may be delivered through **approved communication channels** (Section 3).

There is no “final warning” step, no probation paperwork, and no separate improvement-plan forms in this system. Improvement is expected after coaching and warnings; failure to improve may result in separation.
