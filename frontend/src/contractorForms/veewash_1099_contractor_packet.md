## 4. Service Standards, Warnings, and Removal from Future Assignments

Contractor must follow Company safety, hygiene, customer-property, equipment-use, confidentiality, and premises rules while performing accepted services.

### A. Required Service Performance Standards

Contractor understands that the Company’s laundry service depends on speed, accuracy, safety, and customer-property protection. Contractor must meet the following minimum service standards unless the Company approves a different standard in writing for a specific assignment.

#### 1. Folding Speed Standard

Contractor is expected to fold at a minimum speed of approximately:

**2.5 bags per hour, based on 50 lb bags**, while maintaining proper quality, separation, and customer-property care.

This standard may be reviewed based on bag size, item type, order complexity, quality issues, training period, and actual operating conditions.

#### 2. Washing / Drying / Sorting / Transfer Speed Standards

Contractor is expected to complete routine laundry handling tasks efficiently, including:

- Sorting
- Loading washer
- Transferring from washer to dryer
- Loading dryer
- Unloading dryer
- Moving bags/carts to the correct area

Each routine handling step should generally take **not more than approximately 2 minutes**, unless the order is unusually large, heavy, delicate, damaged, mislabeled, mixed, wet, hazardous, or otherwise requires additional care.

#### 3. Weighing Standard

Contractor is expected to weigh each bag/order efficiently and accurately. Each bag weighing step should generally take **not more than approximately 30 seconds per bag**, unless there is a label issue, order issue, machine issue, customer-property issue, or other reasonable delay.

#### 4. Quality Still Comes First

Speed standards do not allow careless handling, mixed orders, damaged garments, unsafe work, inaccurate weights, ignored labels, or poor customer-property care. Contractor must balance speed with accuracy, safety, hygiene, and quality.

#### 5. Two-Week Review / Probation Period

The first **two weeks** of services are treated as a review/probation period. During this period, the Company will evaluate Contractor’s reliability, speed, quality, attendance/availability, clock record accuracy, customer-property handling, safety, and ability to follow service standards.

If the Company is not satisfied during the two-week review/probation period, the Company may stop offering assignments or terminate this Agreement without additional warnings. Contractor will be paid for approved services actually completed.

#### 6. Warnings After Review Period

After the initial two-week review/probation period, the Company may issue written warnings/notices for performance, reliability, safety, quality, customer-property, clock-record, conduct, or premises-rule issues.

Generally, after **two written warnings/notices**, the Company may stop offering future assignments or terminate this Agreement. However, the Company is not required to issue two warnings for serious issues.

#### 7. Immediate Termination / Removal for Serious Issues

The Company may immediately cancel an assignment, remove Contractor from the premises, stop offering assignments, or terminate this Agreement for serious issues, including theft, threats, violence, intoxication, unsafe conduct, customer-property damage/loss, confidentiality breach, falsified records, failure to report injury/incident, unauthorized persons on premises, or conduct that may harm the Company, customers, workers, equipment, property, or reputation.

#### 8. No Employee Discipline System

Warnings/notices are for documentation, service quality, safety, insurance, and business-record purposes only. Because Contractor is not an employee, the Company is not required to follow an employee progressive discipline process. Warnings/notices do not create employment status, employee handbook rights, or guaranteed future work.

### B. Use of Company Raw Materials, Supplies, and Equipment

Contractor may use Company-approved raw materials, supplies, tools, machines, carts, bags, labels, detergents, softeners, cleaning products, laundry products, printers, scales, and related items **only as needed to perform accepted contract services for the Company**.

Contractor may not use Company materials, supplies, products, equipment, machines, customer property, or business systems for personal use, outside work, personal laundry, third-party work, resale, removal from premises, or any unauthorized purpose.

Use of Company supplies or equipment for approved contract work does not by itself create an employment relationship.

### C. Safety and Hygiene Standards

Contractor must:
1. Work in a safe and hygienic manner.
2. Not perform services while knowingly contagious, impaired, unsafe, or unable to safely perform work.
3. Keep customer orders, garments, bags, and linens separate and protected.
4. Keep customer property away from dirty surfaces, unsafe areas, chemicals, pests, spills, or contamination.
5. Report spills, leaks, wet floors, machine problems, electrical issues, smoke, burning smells, broken equipment, or unsafe conditions immediately.
6. Use caution around washers, dryers, carts, chemicals, hot surfaces, doors, vehicles, stairs, and wet floors.
7. Follow any posted safety rules or manager safety instructions while on Company premises.

### D. Customer Property Standards

Contractor must:
1. Handle all customer property carefully.
2. Keep each customer order separate.
3. Follow labels, tags, tickets, route instructions, and service notes.
4. Immediately report damaged, lost, mixed, stained, wet, moldy, hazardous, sharp, unusual, or questionable items.
5. Not remove customer property from the premises or route except as authorized for accepted services.
6. Not make unauthorized promises to customers about refunds, credits, discounts, delivery times, pricing, or damage claims.

### E. Equipment and Premises Rules

Contractor must:
1. Use Company equipment only for approved services.
2. Not bypass safety features or misuse machines.
3. Not allow unauthorized persons into restricted areas.
4. Not bring friends, family members, children, helpers, or substitutes to perform services without written approval.
5. Keep work areas reasonably clean and free of unnecessary hazards.
6. Follow premises access, closing, security, and camera-area rules.

### F. Confidentiality and Customer Information

Contractor must not misuse, copy, share, sell, disclose, photograph, or distribute customer information, order information, phone numbers, addresses, pricing, Company records, business processes, app data, or other confidential information.

Contractor may use customer and Company information only to perform accepted services.

### G. Clock Records, Invoices, and Payment Records

Contractor must accurately clock in and out, review biweekly invoice/payment summaries, and promptly report any error.

Contractor must not falsify clock records, invoice records, service records, payment records, Health & Safety Credit requests, incident reports, or any Company documentation.

### H. Warnings and Documentation

If Contractor violates service standards, damages customer property, creates a safety risk, fails to complete accepted work, fails to report an incident, mishandles customer information, falsifies records, or behaves in an unprofessional manner, the Company may issue a written warning or written notice.

Any warning or written notice is for documentation, service-quality, safety, insurance, and business-record purposes only.

Because Contractor is not an employee, the Company is not required to follow a progressive discipline process. A written warning does not create an employment relationship, employee status, employee handbook right, or guarantee of future assignments.

Contractor will be paid for approved services actually completed before termination, subject to documented corrections, disputes, or adjustments.

## 5. Premises Break, Meal Period, Rest Area, and Conduct Rules

These premises rules apply whenever Contractor is present at the Company location, whether Contractor is actively performing accepted services, waiting for an assignment, taking a break, taking a meal period, or using any rest area.

### A. Breaks, Meal Periods, and Rest Time

Contractor is responsible for managing Contractor’s own breaks, meal periods, rest time, and availability, subject to accepted assignment timing, customer service needs, safety, and hygiene requirements.

Although Contractor is not an employee, the Company’s premises policy is designed to be generally consistent with New York meal-period standards for covered employees. As a practical safety and compliance standard, if Contractor performs accepted services for more than 6 hours during a day, Contractor should take an uninterrupted meal period of at least 30 minutes, unless a different period is required or approved by applicable law, written policy, or business need.

If Contractor performs accepted services during a long day that begins before 11:00 a.m. and continues after 7:00 p.m., Contractor should take an additional meal/rest period consistent with applicable New York meal-period standards where appropriate.

Contractor is not required to remain on premises during unpaid break or meal time unless Contractor chooses to do so or unless a specific accepted assignment requires Contractor to remain available.

If Contractor takes a break or meal period while performing accepted services, Contractor should clock out or follow the Company’s time-record procedure if the break or meal period is not part of approved paid service time.

Contractor should not claim payment for break time, meal time, personal time, waiting time, or non-service time unless approved in writing or unless the time is treated as approved service time by the Company.

If Contractor is not fully relieved from services during a break or meal period, or if Contractor is asked to continue working during that time, Contractor must notify the Company so the time record/payment summary can be corrected if needed.

### B. Short Rest Breaks

If the Company approves a short rest break while Contractor remains available or continues to perform services, the Company may treat that time as approved service time depending on the circumstances and the payment summary rules.

Contractor should follow the Company’s clock-in/clock-out or time-record instructions so that break time, meal time, and service time are accurately documented.

### C. Rest Area and Food Rules

Contractor may use designated break/rest areas only if available and approved by the Company.

Contractor must keep the area clean and must not leave food, drinks, garbage, personal items, or spills in customer areas, production areas, machine areas, or restricted areas.

Food and drinks should not be placed near customer garments, bags, linens, chemicals, machines, folding tables, POS systems, printers, or business records.

Contractor must dispose of garbage properly and clean up after any meal, snack, or drink.

### D. Premises Conduct Rules

Contractor must:
1. Treat customers, employees, managers, and other contractors respectfully.
2. Avoid loud arguments, abusive language, threats, harassment, intimidation, or disruptive behavior.
3. Avoid sleeping, loitering, or occupying customer areas in a way that disrupts business.
4. Avoid personal visitors, children, friends, or unauthorized helpers on premises without approval.
5. Keep personal belongings in an approved area only.
6. Not use Company supplies, machines, phones, computers, POS systems, customer property, or business records for personal use without approval.
7. Not smoke, vape, drink alcohol, use drugs, or be impaired on Company premises.
8. Not bring weapons, dangerous items, or illegal substances onto Company premises.
9. Follow camera, security, access, opening, closing, and restricted-area rules.
10. Immediately report unsafe conditions, customer complaints, machine problems, injuries, spills, suspicious activity, or incidents.

### E. Violation of Premises Rules

Violation of premises break, meal-period, rest area, conduct, safety, hygiene, or access rules may result in written notice, cancellation of assignment, removal from the premises, non-offer of future assignments, or termination of this Agreement.

Warnings or notices are for documentation and service-quality purposes only and do not create an employment relationship or progressive discipline requirement.

## 7. Contractor Health & Safety Credit Policy

Contractor is not an employee and does not accrue employee sick leave, PTO, vacation, or employee benefits.

However, for safety and reliability, the Company provides a limited **Contractor Health & Safety Credit**.

### Credit Formula

Contractor may earn:

**1 hour of Health & Safety Credit for every 30 completed service hours**, capped at **40 hours per calendar year**.

### Use of Credit

The credit may be used only when Contractor cannot perform accepted services due to:

1. Illness
2. Injury
3. Contagious condition
4. Medical appointment related to illness or injury
5. Safety concern that makes performance unsafe

Contractor must notify the Company as soon as reasonably possible.

### Payment

Approved credit is paid at Contractor’s regular service rate as contractor compensation. No payroll taxes are withheld unless required by law. Contractor remains responsible for taxes.

Unused credit has no cash value and is not paid out at year-end or when the relationship ends.

### No Employee Sick Leave

This policy does not create employee sick leave, employment status, guaranteed work, or employee benefits. It is a contractor safety and reliability accommodation only.

### Misuse

False, misleading, repeated, abusive, or unsupported use of this credit may result in denial of credit, written notice, non-offer of future assignments, or termination of this Agreement.
